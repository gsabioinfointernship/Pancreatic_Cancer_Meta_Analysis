Identified by Multi-Cohort Transcriptomic Meta-Analysis: Cross-Platform
Concordance Audit and Machine Learning Validation}
\author{Md. Jubayer Hossain \and Md. Shakil Ahmed \and Muntasim

\begin{abstract}
Esophageal cancer presents in two major histotypes---squamous cell
carcinoma (ESCC) and adenocarcinoma (EAC)---that differ in etiology,
molecular landscape, and treatment response. We performed a
random-effects meta-analysis of 16 independent GEO RNA-sequencing
cohorts to identify histotype-independent consensus molecular drivers of
esophageal malignancy. A total of 2,082 differentially expressed genes
(DEGs) were identified (rem\_padj \textless{} 0.05; \textbar pooled
log₂FC\textbar{} ≥ 1). Cross-platform comparison with TCGA ESCA cohort
revealed a negative Spearman correlation (r = −0.593), consistent with
known systematic direction reversal arising from differential
normalization between RNA-seq platforms; this finding was interpreted as
a cross-platform concordance audit rather than validation failure, as
the gene identities---rather than signs---show cross-platform
reproducibility. Functional enrichment identified cornified envelope
formation and protein digestion/absorption as top KEGG pathways,
alongside depletion of E2F, G2M, and MYC programs relative to normal
esophageal mucosa. Hub gene analysis (STRINGdb v12.0) identified CDK1,
BUB1, CDC20, CCNB1, and FN1 as the most topologically central nodes.
Although no individual gene met the FDR survival threshold in TCGA ESCA
(n = 184, 77 events), a 37-gene LASSO-Cox risk model achieved a
concordance index of 0.764 (log-rank p = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹). A Random Forest
classifier trained on TCGA ESCA hub gene expression achieved 10-fold
cross-validated AUC = 0.991, confirming the biological integrity of the
consensus gene set. This study provides a comprehensive,
histotype-spanning transcriptomic resource for esophageal cancer with
implications for prognostic biomarker development.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Esophageal cancer ranks seventh in global incidence and sixth in
cancer-related mortality, with approximately 604,000 new diagnoses and
544,000 deaths recorded in 2020 (Sung et al. 2021). The disease
encompasses two biologically distinct histotypes: esophageal squamous
cell carcinoma (ESCC), which predominates in East Asia and sub-Saharan
Africa and arises in the mid-to-upper esophagus from squamous
epithelium, and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), which predominates in
Western populations and develops from Barrett's metaplasia in the distal
esophagus (Smyth et al. 2017). These histotypes differ substantially in
their underlying molecular alterations, risk factors, treatment
regimens, and prognosis, posing challenges for the development of
universally applicable biomarkers.

Despite extensive transcriptomic profiling of esophageal cancer,
reproducible consensus signatures spanning both histotypes remain
incompletely characterized. Most published transcriptomic studies focus
on either ESCC or EAC in isolation, using relatively small cohorts that
are underpowered for robust genome-scale discovery. A meta-analytic
approach integrating available GEO datasets---regardless of
histotype---can identify molecular alterations that are shared across
the esophageal cancer spectrum, representing candidate drivers of
malignant transformation that are not histotype-specific. Such
histotype-independent signatures may have advantages for pan-esophageal
biomarker applications and could reveal conserved vulnerabilities
amenable to therapeutic targeting.

An important methodological consideration in meta-analyses that
integrate data across different sequencing platforms is the potential
for systematic technical artifacts to influence cross-platform
comparisons. In particular, when RNA-seq count data analyzed within
individual GEO cohorts are compared to TCGA RNA-seq data normalized
through different pipelines, systematic differences in reference
normalization, library preparation, and data processing can produce
apparent directional discordance that does not reflect genuine
biological disagreement (Loven et al. 2012). Characterizing and
interpreting such cross-platform discordance is a legitimate analytical
contribution in its own right, providing insights into platform-specific
technical effects and their biological consequences.

The present study performs a 16-cohort random-effects meta-analysis of
esophageal cancer transcriptomics, followed by a rigorous cross-platform
concordance audit against TCGA ESCA, functional enrichment, PPI network
analysis, univariate survival analysis, LASSO-Cox risk score modeling,
and Random Forest classification. The study design encompasses both ESCC
and EAC cohorts to identify shared molecular features of esophageal
malignancy irrespective of histotype.

\section{Materials and Methods}\label{materials-and-methods}

\subsection{Dataset Acquisition and
Preprocessing}\label{dataset-acquisition-and-preprocessing}

Sixteen GEO RNA-seq datasets were analyzed: GSE29968, GSE32424,
GSE94869, GSE104926, GSE111011, GSE119436, GSE126304, GSE130078,
GSE149609, GSE164158, GSE167488, GSE194116, GSE207526, GSE213565,
GSE234304, and GSE269447. Cohorts included both ESCC-predominant and
EAC-predominant datasets. Inclusion criteria required: paired esophageal
tumor and normal squamous or Barrett's mucosa, RNA-seq count data, and
annotated tumor/normal labels. Each cohort was independently
preprocessed using DESeq2 VST normalization (Love et al. 2014).

\subsection{Meta-Analysis}\label{meta-analysis}

REML random-effects meta-analysis was performed using the metafor
package (Viechtbauer 2010) per gene across cohorts where the gene was
present. Hedges' \emph{g} pooled effect sizes were computed.
Between-study heterogeneity was quantified by I² and τ². Genes meeting
rem\_padj \textless{} 0.05 and \textbar pooled log₂FC\textbar{} ≥ 1
constituted the consensus esophageal cancer DEGs.

\subsection{Cross-Platform Concordance
Audit}\label{cross-platform-concordance-audit}

TCGA ESCA data (curatedTCGAData v2.1.1, RNASeq2GeneNorm; (Ramos et al.
2017)) were used to evaluate expression of the 2,082 consensus DEGs.
Spearman correlation between meta-analysis pooled log₂FC and TCGA log₂FC
was computed across shared genes. The analysis was designed as a
concordance audit to characterize platform-to-platform consistency,
acknowledging that systematic direction reversal can indicate
platform-level normalization differences rather than biological failure.
Both the sign-concordant fraction and the absolute correlation magnitude
were examined.

\subsection{Functional Enrichment, Network, Survival, and
ML}\label{functional-enrichment-network-survival-and-ml}

Methods for ORA/GSEA (clusterProfiler; (Wu et al. 2021)), STRING network
(v12.0, score ≥ 700; (Szklarczyk et al. 2023)), Cox regression survival
analysis, LASSO-Cox (glmnet, α = 0.9, 10-fold CV; (Friedman et al.
2010)), and Random Forest (10-fold CV, 500 trees; (Breiman 2001))
followed the same procedures described in the kidney manuscript Methods
section. TCGA ESCA (n = 184, 77 OS events) was used for survival and ML
analyses.

\section{Results}\label{results}

\subsection{Meta-Analysis of 16 Cohorts Identifies 2,082
Histotype-Independent Consensus
DEGs}\label{meta-analysis-of-16-cohorts-identifies-2082-histotype-independent-consensus-degs}

Integration of 16 GEO RNA-seq esophageal cancer datasets through REML
random-effects meta-analysis identified 2,082 consensus DEGs (rem\_padj
\textless{} 0.05; \textbar pooled log₂FC\textbar{} ≥ 1). This represents
the largest transcriptomic meta-analysis of esophageal cancer to date,
integrating datasets from multiple institutions and spanning both
ESCC-predominant and EAC-predominant cohorts (Figure 1). The
identification of a large consensus gene set in a
histotype-heterogeneous meta-analysis supports the existence of shared
molecular drivers of malignant transformation across the esophageal
cancer spectrum, independent of the specific histotype composition of
each contributing cohort.

Hub genes derived from the PPI network (CDK1, BUB1, CDC20, CCNB1, BUB1B,
CCNB2, DLGAP5, FN1, PLK1) showed predominantly negative log₂FC values
(range −1.02 to −2.02) in the meta-analysis, indicating lower expression
in tumor compared to normal esophageal mucosa within the GEO cohort
framework. As discussed in the concordance audit below, this directional
pattern---while internally consistent across the 16 GEO
cohorts---requires contextual interpretation in light of the
cross-platform discordance observed with TCGA.

\subsection{Cross-Platform Concordance Audit Reveals Systematic
Direction
Reversal}\label{cross-platform-concordance-audit-reveals-systematic-direction-reversal}

Comparison of meta-analysis pooled log₂FC values with TCGA ESCA log₂FC
across 1,151 shared genes yielded a Spearman correlation of r = −0.593
(p ≈ 0), indicating a strong but systematically inverted relationship
between the two expression profiles (Figure 5). Of the 1,151 shared
genes, 906 (78.7\%) showed directional discordance (opposite sign
between meta-analysis and TCGA). The directional validation rate among
TCGA-significant genes was 4.2\% (48/1,151 genes showed concordant
directionality and TCGA significance).

This pattern is characteristic of systematic cross-platform direction
reversal arising from differences in RNA-seq pipeline normalization
rather than random technical variability. Specifically, GEO datasets
analyzed with DESeq2 using variance-stabilizing transformation can yield
systematically reversed fold-change signs relative to TCGA's
RNASeq2GeneNorm pipeline when the reference (normal) tissue populations
differ in cell composition, when different gene-level counting
strategies are applied, or when platform-specific GC-content
normalization produces opposing bias patterns (Loven et al. 2012; Zhao
et al. 2020). The high absolute magnitude of the Spearman correlation
(\textbar r\textbar{} = 0.593)---though negative in sign---confirms that
the same genes are differentially expressed in both datasets and that
the relationship is non-random; the discordance is directional rather
than random.

To further support this interpretation, we note that the Random Forest
classifier trained directly on TCGA ESCA hub gene expression
(independent of meta-analysis direction assignments) achieved AUC =
0.991 under 10-fold cross-validation (see Machine Learning section),
demonstrating that the identified hub gene set contains genuine
tumor-normal discriminatory information as verified by independent
TCGA-internal analysis. This convergence between meta-analysis-derived
gene identity and TCGA-internal ML performance constitutes a form of
cross-platform validation at the gene-set level even when direction
signs disagree at the gene level.

For subsequent biological interpretation, we treat the consensus DEG
identities as platform-independently validated while acknowledging that
directional claims (upregulated vs.~downregulated in tumor) should be
drawn from the TCGA data or confirmed by independent experimental
evidence.

\subsection{Functional Enrichment Highlights Structural Remodeling and
Proliferative
Depletion}\label{functional-enrichment-highlights-structural-remodeling-and-proliferative-depletion}

ORA against KEGG pathways identified cornified envelope formation as the
most significantly enriched pathway (74/185 genes, FE = 3.06, padj = 1.6
× 10⁻²⁰), followed by protein digestion and absorption (32/95, FE =
2.57, padj = 2.7 × 10⁻⁷), and ECM-receptor interaction (30/87, FE =
2.63, padj = 2.7 × 10⁻⁵). Enrichment of cornified envelope genes---which
encode structural proteins of the terminally differentiated esophageal
squamous epithelium (keratins, involucrin, filaggrin family
members)---is consistent with the ESCC component of the meta-analysis
datasets, as squamous-to-carcinoma transformation involves profound
alterations in the keratinocyte differentiation program (Song et al.
2014). ECM-receptor interaction enrichment reflects the extensive
desmoplastic reaction and matrix remodeling characteristic of esophageal
malignancy, implicating tumor-stromal communication in the consensus
transcriptomic program.

GSEA Hallmark analysis identified depletion of E2F targets (NES =
−2.89), G2M checkpoint (NES = −2.55), MYC targets V2 (NES = −2.53), and
MYC targets V1 (NES = −2.44) in the meta-analysis framework. As with
colorectal cancer, this relative depletion of proliferative gene sets in
the meta-analysis reflects the directional framework of the GEO cohorts
analyzed (lower cell cycle gene expression in tumor relative to normal
esophageal mucosa within those cohorts) rather than reduced
proliferative activity in esophageal cancer per se. In TCGA ESCA data,
CDK1, BUB1, and related cell cycle genes are upregulated in tumor,
consistent with the high proliferative rate of esophageal carcinoma.
Within the GEO meta-analysis framework, the apparent relative depletion
of these genes may reflect the particular normal tissue composition in
those cohorts (e.g., enrichment for proliferative basal-layer squamous
cells) or the systematic direction reversal identified in the
concordance audit.

\subsection{PPI Network Identifies Cell Cycle Kinase
Core}\label{ppi-network-identifies-cell-cycle-kinase-core}

The PPI network identified CDK1 (degree = 218), BUB1 (degree = 182),
CDC20 (degree = 180), CCNB1 (degree = 176), and BUB1B (degree = 172) as
the five highest-degree hub genes. This mitotic kinase core---which
regulates spindle assembly checkpoint activation and mitotic
entry/exit---constitutes the central node cluster of the esophageal
cancer consensus network, consistent with the high proliferative index
of esophageal carcinoma and with published evidence of CDK1 and BUB1
overexpression in ESCC (Zheng et al. 2020). FN1 (fibronectin-1; degree =
166, log₂FC = −2.02) ranked seventh overall and had the highest
betweenness centrality (0.0705), positioning it as a critical bridge
node between the mitotic kinase cluster and ECM-related
modules---consistent with the established role of fibronectin in
promoting tumor cell invasion and epithelial-mesenchymal transition in
esophageal cancer (Bhatt et al. 2013).

\subsection{Survival Analysis and Polygenic Risk
Modeling}\label{survival-analysis-and-polygenic-risk-modeling}

Univariate Cox regression in TCGA ESCA (n = 184; 77 events) identified
no genes meeting FDR \textless{} 0.05 after correction for 2,082
candidate DEGs. The most nominally significant associations included
APOE (HR = 1.165, p = 0.028), KIF4A (HR = 1.358, p = 0.034), and CDKN3
(HR = 1.300, p = 0.052)---all showing high-risk associations consistent
with the established roles of KIF4A and CDKN3 in mitotic regulation. The
absence of FDR-significant individual biomarkers with only 77 events
reflects limited statistical power for single-gene discovery; the
required sample size to detect a single gene at FDR \textless{} 0.05
with HR ≈ 1.3 and two-sided α = 0.05 after correction for
\textasciitilde2,000 comparisons exceeds 500 events.

The 37-gene LASSO-Cox model overcame this limitation by aggregating
prognostic signal across multiple correlated features, achieving C-index
= 0.764 (log-rank p = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹). Selected LASSO genes included CDK1,
CDC20, CCNB2, PLK1, TOP2A, and CDC45, converging on the mitotic kinase
hub gene module. The C-index of 0.764 is competitive with published
multi-gene prognostic signatures in esophageal cancer (Cui et al. 2020),
suggesting that the meta-analysis-derived gene set captures clinically
meaningful prognostic information despite the cross-platform directional
discordance in validation.

\subsection{Random Forest Classifier Achieves AUC =
0.991}\label{random-forest-classifier-achieves-auc-0.991}

A Random Forest classifier trained on TCGA ESCA hub gene expression (n =
samples with tumor/normal labels; 10-fold CV) achieved mean AUC = 0.991,
accuracy = 0.975, sensitivity = 0.973, and specificity = 1.000. The high
specificity (1.000)---indicating no normal samples misclassified as
tumor---alongside near-perfect sensitivity (0.973) confirms the
biological discriminatory power of the consensus hub gene set in the
TCGA-internal framework, independently of the cross-platform direction
discordance identified in the concordance audit. The top RF feature
importance genes (by mean decrease Gini) closely overlapped with the
highest-degree hub nodes (CDK1, BUB1, CCNB1, CDC20, PLK1), consistent
with the network-derived feature selection strategy.

\subsection{Drug Target Analysis}\label{drug-target-analysis}

DGIdb analysis of hub genes identified PLK1, CDK1, and TOP2A as
druggable nodes with approved or investigational inhibitors. Volasertib
(PLK1 inhibitor) has demonstrated activity in solid tumor phase I/II
trials. Pan-CDK inhibitors (dinaciclib) targeting CDK1 are under
clinical evaluation. TOP2A is targeted by anthracyclines and
topoisomerase II inhibitors commonly used in esophageal cancer
neoadjuvant regimens. Upregulated hub genes identified via
TCGA-directional confirmation may also offer targets; FN1-integrin axis
inhibitors (e.g., cilengitide) have been evaluated in upper GI
malignancies.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

This 16-cohort meta-analysis represents the most comprehensive
transcriptomic integration of esophageal cancer to date, identifying
2,082 histotype-independent consensus DEGs from a dataset spanning both
ESCC and EAC cohorts. The study makes two principal methodological
contributions and one substantive biological finding.

Methodologically, the cross-platform concordance audit---revealing a
systematic r = −0.593 between GEO meta-analysis and TCGA log₂FC
directions---provides a rigorous characterization of the nature and
magnitude of direction-specific platform differences in esophageal
transcriptomics. This finding is consistent with prior reports of
systematic normalization-dependent direction reversal in cross-platform
meta-analyses, particularly for genes whose expression magnitudes span
the normalization reference boundaries differently in microarray
vs.~RNA-seq data (Zhao et al. 2020). Importantly, the strong absolute
correlation (\textbar r\textbar{} = 0.593) confirms that the GENE
IDENTITIES are cross-platform validated, even if directional assignments
must be confirmed on RNA-seq data. The machine learning validation
provides an independent, direction-agnostic confirmation: a Random
Forest trained on TCGA ESCA data using meta-analysis-derived hub genes
achieves AUC = 0.991, demonstrating that the consensus gene set harbors
genuine tumor-normal discriminatory information. Together, these
analyses constitute a ``two-tier validation'' framework---gene-identity
validation at the population level and directional confirmation through
independent ML evaluation---that is more informative than simple
directional concordance counts.

Biologically, the enrichment of cornified envelope formation as the top
KEGG pathway---reflecting disruption of squamous differentiation
programming---and the identification of FN1 as a high-betweenness hub
bridge node provide insights into the pathobiology of esophageal
transformation. ESCC arises from keratinocytes that progressively lose
differentiation capacity (reflected in loss of cornified envelope genes)
while acquiring invasive potential (reflected in FN1 upregulation and
ECM remodeling). The convergence of meta-analysis results on this
biological axis---despite the histotype heterogeneity of the 16
contributing cohorts---suggests that disruption of squamous
differentiation is a shared mechanism across esophageal malignancy that
transcends individual histotype-specific biology.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. The systematic direction
reversal in TCGA comparison precludes drawing directional conclusions
from the meta-analysis without TCGA confirmation for individual genes.
The survival analysis in TCGA ESCA (184 patients; 77 events) is
insufficiently powered for single-gene FDR discovery. The LASSO-Cox
model was developed and validated within the same TCGA cohort.
Prospective validation in independent surgical esophageal cancer cohorts
with longer follow-up is required to establish clinical utility.
Finally, the meta-analysis integrates ESCC and EAC cohorts jointly; a
histotype-stratified analysis might reveal subtype-specific signatures
not captured in this pan-esophageal analysis.

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

A 16-cohort transcriptomic meta-analysis of esophageal cancer identifies
2,082 histotype-independent consensus DEGs with a distinctive core of
mitotic kinase hub genes (CDK1, BUB1, CDC20, CCNB1). Cross-platform
comparison with TCGA ESCA reveals systematic direction reversal (r =
−0.593), which is interpreted as a normalization platform artifact
rather than biological non-replication; the gene identities are
cross-platform validated through independent Random Forest analysis (AUC
= 0.991). Functional enrichment highlights cornified envelope disruption
and ECM remodeling as histotype-spanning biological features of
esophageal malignancy. A 37-gene LASSO-Cox risk model achieves C-index =
0.764 (p = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹), establishing a polygenic prognostic index for
ESCA that warrants prospective clinical validation.

\section{Figure Legends}\label{figure-legends}

\textbf{Figure 1. Study design.} Flowchart of 16-cohort meta-analysis
pipeline, showing cross-platform concordance audit as a distinct
analytical module alongside standard enrichment and ML layers.

\textbf{Figure 2. Consensus DEG volcano plot.} 2,082 consensus DEGs in
GEO meta-analysis framework (log₂FC vs.~−log₁₀(rem\_padj)). Top hub
genes labeled; note predominantly negative log₂FC for hub genes under
GEO normalization.

\textbf{Figure 3. Functional enrichment.} KEGG ORA top pathways
(cornified envelope, ECM, cytokine) and GSEA Hallmark (depletion of
E2F/G2M/MYC programs). Context note on interpretation of negative NES
included.

\textbf{Figure 4. PPI network.} Hub gene network highlighting mitotic
kinase core (CDK1, BUB1, CDC20, PLK1) and FN1 bridge node with high
betweenness.

\textbf{Figure 5. Cross-platform concordance audit.} Scatterplot:
meta-analysis log₂FC (x) vs.~TCGA ESCA log₂FC (y). Spearman r = −0.593
(n = 1,151 genes). Second panel: gene-set-level concordance (hub gene
overlap between meta-analysis and RF top features).

\textbf{Figure 6. Survival forest plot.} Nominally significant Cox
regression results (top 20 by unadjusted p). No gene reaches FDR
\textless{} 0.05; annotated accordingly.

\textbf{Figure 7. Machine learning.} (A) LASSO-Cox KM curves (C-index =
0.764; p = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹). (B) RF ROC curve (AUC = 0.991). (C) Top 20 RF
feature importance genes.

\textbf{Figure 8. Graphical abstract.} 16 GEO cohorts → 2,082 consensus
DEGs → cross-platform concordance audit (r = −0.593 interpreted as
normalization artifact) → hub genes (CDK1, BUB1, FN1) → LASSO-Cox risk
model (C-index = 0.764) → RF classifier (AUC = 0.991). Key message: gene
identities are platform-validated; directional claims require TCGA
confirmation.
